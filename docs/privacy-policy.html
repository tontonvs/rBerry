rBerry Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 4, 2026

rBerry (shown as "RetroBerry" on the loading screen) is an Android app by Noven that plays classic keypad-phone Java (J2ME) games inside a retro handheld-style interface. This page explains what the app does with your information. The short version: the developer does not collect your data.

What we collect

Nothing. rBerry has no accounts, no sign-in, no advertising, no analytics and no crash-report uploader. The developer does not receive any information from the app, and nothing is sent to the developer's servers because there are none.

What stays on your device

  • Games you import. When you pick a .jar or .jad file, the app copies it into its own storage on your device so it can run it. Your games' save data stays there too.
  • Settings. Display layout, sound and vibration choices, and similar preferences are stored on your device only.

Uninstalling the app removes this data.

Permissions

  • Storage (Android 9 and older only): to read and keep your game files. Newer Android versions use the file picker and need no storage permission.
  • Vibration: for the optional key-press feedback.
  • Internet: only used when a game you run makes its own network requests, or when you open a link to install a game. rBerry itself does not connect to any server.
  • Shortcut creation: to place a game shortcut on your home screen if you ask for one.

rBerry does not ask for access to your camera, microphone, location, Bluetooth, contacts or notifications.

Games you import

Games are made by other people. A game may use the internet in its own way, for example for online scores, and rBerry cannot control what it sends. Only import games from sources you trust. rBerry does not include or distribute commercial games.

Google Play

If you install rBerry from Google Play, Google may collect crash and performance information from devices where the user has chosen to share diagnostics. That is handled by Google under its own privacy policy. The developer sees only summary reports in the Play Console.

Children

rBerry is a general-purpose app and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Open source

rBerry is based on the open-source J2ME Loader (Apache License 2.0). The source code is at github.com/tontonvs/rBerry.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
